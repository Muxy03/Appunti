# Fundamental principles of interaction

Fundamental psychological principles:
1. affordances
2. signifiers
3. constraints
4. mappings
5. feedback
6. conceptual model of the system

## 1. AFFORDANCES

The term affordance refers to the relationship between a physical object and a person.

Whether an affordance exists depends upon the properties of both the object and the agent

Anti-affordance -> spuntoni anti piccioni

To be effective, affordances and anti-affordances have to be: discoverable and perceivable.

## 2. SIGNIFIERS

The affordance of touching exists on the entire screen: 

That’s not the same thing as saying what action is possible.

Affordances determine what actions are possible. 

Signifiers communicate where the action should take place.

![[Pasted image 20241012123114.png]]

Signifiers can be deliberate and intentional, such as the sign push on a door

they may also be accidental and unintentional, such as our use of the visible trail made by previous people walking through a field or over a snow-covered terrain to determine the best path.

Affordances represent the possibilities in the world for how an agent can interact with something. Some affordances are perceivable, others are invisible. 

Signifiers are signals. Some signifiers are signs, labels, and drawings placed in the world

Some signifiers are simply the perceived affordances, such as the handle of a door

## 3. MAPPING

Mapping means the relationship between the elements of two sets of things.

Mapping is an important concept in the design and layout of controls and displays. When the mapping uses spatial correspondence between the layout of the controls and the devices being controlled, it is easy to determine how to use them.

Natural mapping (taking advantage of spatial analogies) leads to immediate understanding.

7 principi fondamentali of design:
- Discoverability = **It is possible to determine what actions are possible and the current state of the device**
- Feedback = **There is full and continuous information about the results of actions and the current state of the product or service. After an action has been executed, it is easy to determine the new state.**
- Conceptual model = **The design projects all the information needed to create a good conceptual model of the system, leading to understanding and a feeling of control. The conceptual model enhances both discoverability and evaluation of results.**
- Affordances = **The proper affordances exist to make the desired actions possible**
- Signifiers = **Effective use of signifiers ensures discoverability and that the feedback is well communicated and intelligible.**
- Mappings = **The relationship between controls and their actions follows the principles of good mapping, enhanced as much as possible through spatial layout and temporal contiguity.**
- Constraints = **Providing physical, logical, semantic, and cultural constraints guides actions and eases interpretation.**

Hidden information can be valuable for decluttering the interface, providing context-sensitive options, and revealing advanced features when users require them. However, if not well-organized, it can lead to discoverability issues, where users struggle to find and utilize hidden features.
Modes example: in a drawing application, there might be different modes for drawing, selecting objects, and applying text. Each mode alters the behavior of the interface, allowing users to perform distinct actions.
Modes can increase the versatility of the system, but they can also introduce confusion, especially if users inadvertently switch modes or struggle to determine the current mode.

Ambiguity arises because users may inadvertently switch modes or be unsure of the current mode. Complexity increases as more modes are introduced, making the system harder to learn and master.

Movements of the pointing device are echoed on the screen by movements of the pointer

Fitts's Law = the time required to rapidly move to a target area is a function of the ratio between the distance (D) to the target and the width of the target (W).

$$
MT = a+b*ID=a+b*log_2(\frac{2D}{W})
$$

MT = Movement time
a = time to start/stop in seconds
b = inherent speed of device
D = distance from starting point to center of target
W = width of the target measured along the axis of motion

Pointng devices classification:
- direct input  = the on-screen pointer is at the same physical position as the pointing device
- indirect input = is not at the same physical position as the pointer
- absolute movement = provides a consistent mapping between a point in the input space and a point in the output space
- relative movement = maps displacement in the input space to displacement in the output state
- isotonic = movable amnd measures its displacement
- isometric = fixed and measures the force which acts on it
- elastic  = increases its force resistance with displacement
- position control = directly changes the absolute or relaative position og the on-screen pointer.

# PROTOTYPING AND MOCKUPS

mockups = a high-fidelity representation of the product that includes more visual detail and design elements than a wireframe

A realistic visual design that resembles what the new product or functionality will look like.

prototype = is an experimental and early sample of a design that allows users to visualize and interact with it before a final product is developed. Teams build prototypes of varying degrees of fidelity to capture design concepts and test on users

So, prototyping is the process in which design teams ideate, experiment with, and bring concepts to life. Is the phase in which design teams implement ideas into tangible forms from paper to digital.

Prototyping allows designers to test their ideas on users and refine their designs based on feedback. This can help save time and resources by identifying potential issues early on.

![[Pasted image 20241203235056.png]]

A prototype is a design solution candidate that addresses specific problems/needs.
With it, usability problems can be identified before committing to the development of the final design.

prototypes fidelty = How much a prototype resembles the final product (in look, feel and function)

low fidelty -> fatto su carta

6 dimensioni della prototypes' fidelity:
- visual/Physical realism = How closely the prototype resembles the final product in terms of visual design and aesthetics.
- scope = The breadth and depth of the design represented in the prototype.
- functionality = What actually works in the prototype, such as links or buttons in a web app.
- Data = Whether the prototype operates on real or fake data.
- Autonomy = Whether the prototype can operate alone or requires input from the designer or user.
- Platform = Whether the prototype is an interim or final implementation.

Tecniche di prototype:
- paper prototypes = A paper prototype is a low-fidelity prototype created on paper or cardboard that represents the basic layout and functionality of a product or service. -> can be used to quickly test a wide range of design elements, from the placement of buttons and menus to the flow of information and user interactions.
- (mid-fi) wireframe prototypes = A wireframe prototype is a digital prototype that represents the basic layout and functionality of a product or service, without much detail or visual design.
- wizard of Oz prototypes = A Wizard of Oz is a prototype that simulates the behavior of a product or service using human input instead of actual technology.
- functional prototypes = A functional prototype is a prototype that includes working or partially working features. It is used to test and refine the technical aspects of a product, such as the responsiveness and functionality of different features. -> They are particularly useful in the later stages of the design process, when designers are looking to fine-tune the technical details of their designs before moving on to full development.
- non-functional prototypes = A non-functional prototype is a prototype that looks and feels like a real product or system but does not have any functional capabilities. It is typically used to demonstrate the physical design and user interface of a product or system, without the need for any actual working components or systems. -> testing UX or testing visual design.
