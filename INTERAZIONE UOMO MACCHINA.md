# Fundamental principles of interaction

Fundamental psychological principles:
1. affordances
2. signifiers
3. constraints
4. mappings
5. feedback
6. conceptual model of the system

## 1. AFFORDANCES

The term affordance refers to the relationship between a physical object and a person.

Whether an affordance exists depends upon the properties of both the object and the agent

Anti-affordance -> spuntoni anti piccioni

To be effective, affordances and anti-affordances have to be: discoverable and perceivable.

## 2. SIGNIFIERS

The affordance of touching exists on the entire screen: 

That’s not the same thing as saying what action is possible.

Affordances determine what actions are possible. 

Signifiers communicate where the action should take place.

![[Pasted image 20241012123114.png]]

Signifiers can be deliberate and intentional, such as the sign push on a door

they may also be accidental and unintentional, such as our use of the visible trail made by previous people walking through a field or over a snow-covered terrain to determine the best path.

Affordances represent the possibilities in the world for how an agent can interact with something. Some affordances are perceivable, others are invisible. 

Signifiers are signals. Some signifiers are signs, labels, and drawings placed in the world

Some signifiers are simply the perceived affordances, such as the handle of a door

## 3. MAPPING

Mapping means the relationship between the elements of two sets of things.

Mapping is an important concept in the design and layout of controls and displays. When the mapping uses spatial correspondence between the layout of the controls and the devices being controlled, it is easy to determine how to use them.

Natural mapping (taking advantage of spatial analogies) leads to immediate understanding.

7 principi fondamentali of design:
- Discoverability = **It is possible to determine what actions are possible and the current state of the device**
- Feedback = **There is full and continuous information about the results of actions and the current state of the product or service. After an action has been executed, it is easy to determine the new state.**
- Conceptual model = **The design projects all the information needed to create a good conceptual model of the system, leading to understanding and a feeling of control. The conceptual model enhances both discoverability and evaluation of results.**
- Affordances = **The proper affordances exist to make the desired actions possible**
- Signifiers = **Effective use of signifiers ensures discoverability and that the feedback is well communicated and intelligible.**
- Mappings = **The relationship between controls and their actions follows the principles of good mapping, enhanced as much as possible through spatial layout and temporal contiguity.**
- Constraints = **Providing physical, logical, semantic, and cultural constraints guides actions and eases interpretation.**

The "Principle of Least Astonishment" (“minor stupore”) is a design principle that suggests that when designing user interfaces or systems, you should minimize the extent to which users are surprised or astonished by the behavior of the system.

![[Pasted image 20241028004347.png]]

Hidden information can be valuable for decluttering the interface, providing context-sensitive options, and revealing advanced features when users require them. However, if not well-organized, it can lead to discoverability issues, where users struggle to find and utilize hidden features.
Modes example: in a drawing application, there might be different modes for drawing, selecting objects, and applying text. Each mode alters the behavior of the interface, allowing users to perform distinct actions.
Modes can increase the versatility of the system, but they can also introduce confusion, especially if users inadvertently switch modes or struggle to determine the current mode.

![[Pasted image 20241028004753.png]]

Ambiguity arises because users may inadvertently switch modes or be unsure of the current mode. Complexity increases as more modes are introduced, making the system harder to learn and master.

**Modes ambiguity: in a symbolic interface style word processor, “d” stands for “delete” or for the letter “d”**
